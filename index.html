About

I'm an applied economist working at the intersection of development economics and labour markets. I completed a year as a Pre-Doctoral Research Fellow under Prof. Nalin Bharti (DPIIT IPR Chair) at IIT Patna, and a subsequent term as Project Associate at IIT Madras under the NPTEL Post Baccalaureate Fellowship, through July 2026. I'm currently continuing this research independently, extending the empirical work developed during those fellowships. My core work uses causal identification methods on Indian state-panel and survey data — spatial Difference-in-Differences and Synthetic Control show that renewable-energy investment in India's coal-dependent states raised formal employment by 4.2–6.8 percentage points over five years, while coal phase-down regions saw real wages fall 7.3–9.1 points with limited recovery. A related paper on Jharkhand's resource-curse dynamics, using System GMM with geological coal reserves as an instrument, finds that education amplifies rather than offsets the resource curse — a talent-misallocation result consistent with Murphy, Shleifer & Vishny's rent-seeking framework. I hold an M.Sc. in Economics from Central University of Andhra Pradesh (CGPA 8.89/10, First Class with Distinction) and am UGC-NET qualified. My toolkit is STATA, EViews, and SPSS, applied to staggered DiD, Synthetic Control, GMM, and DCC-GARCH estimation.
